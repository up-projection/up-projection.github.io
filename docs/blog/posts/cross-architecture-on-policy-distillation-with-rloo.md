# Cross-architecture On-Policy Distillation with REINFORCE Leave-One-Out

I am interested in how far the simplest RL setup can still provide good results: fully on-policy, no KL-div penalty, no importance ratio so no clipping, and using maybe a raw SGD without momentum to reduce overall GPU memory. Doing so is not easy, since each simplification introduces xxx. Removing the KL-Div exposes your RL objective to reward hacking. Using raw REINFORCE with ... So why not choosing security and using complex and vilidated algorithms like PPO-like RL algos and AdamW? Because these methods have been developped before the LLM era and ... easiy the nice French translation skills of Qwen3-4B into a much smaller model, into SmolLM3-360M for example. This small model works when translating very simple texts but strugle with more complex ones, where sometimes it fails to follow the translation instructions.

Distillation is the right approach for this kind objectives: you teach the small model (SmolLM3-360M) to acts as the teacher (Qwen3-4B). I wrote back in 2024 a blogpost on LLM compression approaches, with a big part on distillation:

Basically, there are three different ways to distill:

1. **Logits Distillation**: the student learns directly to mimmick the probability distribution of the teacher (using KL-Divergence loss for example)
2. **Feature Distillation**: the student learns to mimick the intermediate activations (from linear layers, transformer layers, etc.) produced by the teacher.
3. **Sequence-Level Distillation**: the student is trained on synthetic texts generated by the teacher

If the teacher's targets are being generated while the student is training, we call it **online** distillation as opposed to **offline** distillation.
But in both offline and online distillation the student is passive, as it doesn't act and just receive the teacher data. This creates a inference-training mismatch since during inference the teacher is not available. 

Here is where On-policy Distillation comes in: the student (policy) is active during training - it generates its own answers and receives feedback from the teacher. It is akin to Reinforcement Learning: the student generates the answer, and the teacher provides a reward defined by the KL divergence between the student’s probabilities and the teacher’s probabilities on the student generated answer. This have been proven to provide more robust student models and generally perform better.

When using Logits KL-Divergence as reward in On-policy Distillation, it requires to have the same probability distribution space for both teacher and student (they must have the same vocabulare/token size in the `lm_head`)
